Regardless of the role you're interviewing for, one question you'll almost always want to ask is: "Tell me about your past experience."

However, this question can be quite vague, and you'll get all kinds of answers, which might not be what you're after.

I think what you really want when you ask this question is for the candidate to:

- Demonstrate a complex project or product they worked on
- Show how close they were to the real work
- Show you that their experience is relevant to the role you're hiring for

If this is what you're after, here's how I would approach this question.

## How to ask the question

1. **Set up the question.** Ask them to describe one initiative they led or were heavily involved in. Give two ground rules up front:
   - It must be customer or external-facing, not internal. Internal projects usually have far more leeway
   - It can't be a one-dimensional project. It must have both breadth and depth
2. **Ask them to frame the logistics** first:
   - **When it took place:** this helps you frame how to evaluate what they did (e.g. building an agentic harness in 2025 is very different than building one in 2026)
   - **How long it ran:** this helps you gauge if what they're about to tell you sounds reasonable based on your expectations of speed
   - **Who was involved, and their contributions vs others:** this clarifies how they fit into the puzzle, and what work they actually did
3. **Let them tell the high-level story** without interrupting
4. **Dig in where it gets hard.** As the story unfolds, find the moments of complexity or impact and ask them to [double-click](/how-to-get-honest-answers-in-interviews/). Push for exact details about the customer, the approach and the team dynamic.
5. **Ask for reflection:** "If you did this all over again, what would you do differently, and why?"

## How to evaluate the answer

Grade the candidate on [a 1-4 scale](/simple-candidate-scoring-system/) for each of the following high-level dimensions. It's important to keep the grading rubric high-level, as specific details can trap a good candidate into "missing a checkbox".

1. **Ownership.** Were they truly as involved as they say?
   - Their ownership is clear and separate from the team's, and it fits the team size and timeline
2. **Depth.** Can they recall exact details when you double-click?
   - Numbers, decisions, and why the alternatives lost. Vague answers on the parts they claim to own are a red flag
3. **Breadth.** Do they grasp the whole situation, not just their slice?
   - They can explain the customer, the approach and the team dynamic
4. **Reflection.** Is their answer on what they'd change specific and honest?
   - A real lesson with a reason. A lot of people take this opportunity to humble brag instead
